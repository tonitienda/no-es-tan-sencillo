# Episode 01 — decisions and open questions

**Last discussion captured:** 2026-11-04.  
**Status:** Working notes; only the editorial principles below are adopted, not all plot choices.

## Direction agreed or strongly established
- Channel mission: reduce polarization, improve thinking and question oversimplified economic claims without predetermined conclusions.
- Tell a compelling story through Pepi (owner) and Laura (exceptionally skilled employee), not a straw-man debate.
- The core insight is that **creating a job, creating value and deciding distribution are different questions**.
- Use a shift in perspective when Laura later becomes a co-owner and employs others; this does not prove she was wrong before.
- Respect both the entrepreneur's contribution and the employee's contribution, and retain serious structural critiques (capital, options, power).
- Keep the narrative ahead of visual production, and distinguish fictional illustration from empirical research.

## Proposed, still to decide
1. **Conflict level:** cooperative throughout or genuine but respectful disagreement over pay/profits/ownership? Preference expressed in discussion: real disagreement.
2. **Ownership mechanics:** does Pepi offer a stake? Is it purchased, earned through a negotiated arrangement or financed? A 50% transfer cannot be treated casually.
3. **Laura's choice:** employee with a higher salary vs partner, and what each choice says (or does *not* say).
4. **Ending:** a cook or waiter revisits Laura's former challenge; show Laura genuinely wrestling with it rather than a neat moral reversal.
5. **Pacing:** existing 7–9 minute target is provisional; script length should follow story and evidence.
6. **Financial model:** realistic but explicitly hypothetical costs, wages, owner labor, invested funds, financing, growth and failure scenarios. Validate plausibility before using numbers.
7. **Empirical support:** separate observed facts from narrative assumptions; review arguments adverse to both initial interpretations.

## Suggested next steps
1. Review narrative v0.2 and settle how strong Pepi and Laura's conflict should be.
2. Write scenes/dialogue and identify claims that require supporting evidence.
3. Build a transparent bar model and stress-test counterfactuals.
4. Run an adversarial editorial review.
5. Write final voiceover, then plan the reusable visual scenes.

# Pepi y Laura — narrative v0.2

**Status:** Working story draft for discussion, not a production-ready script.  
**Working question:** ¿Quién crea realmente un puesto de trabajo?  
**Target:** Approximately 7–9 minutes, adjustable after scripting and research.

## Story objective
Use a single small bar to investigate three connected but different questions:
1. Who creates and sustains employment?
2. Who creates economic value?
3. How should the resulting income and profits be distributed?

The viewer should have reasons to reconsider an initial intuition more than once. The goal is understanding, not declaring either the owner or the employee right. Neither character is a proxy for an ideology or a villain.

## Act I — The bar that does not yet exist
An empty premises in a neighborhood. Pepi notices an opportunity, saves and invests, negotiates a lease, buys equipment, secures permits, organizes the operation and opens a bar. Revenue is uncertain. She hires Laura.

**Question:** Who created Laura's job? Pepi initiated and funded the business, but sustained demand from customers matters too. Without this particular bar, we cannot claim Laura would have remained unemployed.

## Act II — Laura changes the business
Laura is exceptionally good at her work: remembers customers, improves service, builds relationships and gives people a reason to return. Sales improve, potentially transforming a barely viable business into a profitable one.

**Question:** How much of the additional value does Laura create? How much of the gain belongs to her, and how could we know? Do not equate turnover with profit or assert numerical attribution without evidence or a labeled simulation.

## Act III — The conversation
After closing, Laura asks for a raise and asks why the remaining profits belong to Pepi. Pepi acknowledges Laura's contribution, but the conversation reveals ownership, prior investment, organizational work, uncertainty and bargaining power.

**Provisional dialogue (not final voiceover):**
- Laura: «El bar está funcionando mucho mejor desde que llegué».
- Pepi: «Sí. Y buena parte es gracias a ti».
- Laura: «Entonces, ¿por qué los beneficios son tuyos?»

Pepi raises the possibility of sharing ownership. The terms are **not** assumed to be free or affordable: buying a stake, the valuation, financing and liability need consideration. Laura may prefer higher pay or may be constrained by access to capital. Her choice alone cannot establish whether the initial arrangement was fair.

## Act IV — Two possible futures
**A — Laura stays an employee.** She negotiates better compensation and focuses on the work she values; Pepi retains ownership and its responsibilities.

**B — Laura becomes a partner.** They negotiate a substantial ownership stake (50% can be an illustrative option, not an unexplained gift). Laura participates in decisions, returns and financial exposure according to the actual terms.

With favorable conditions, both illustrated branches can grow and hire two people. These are hypothetical branches, not evidence that ownership structure causes equal growth. A third potential outcome—stagnation or failure—should remain visible in the economic model.

## Act V — The question returns
Focus on branch B. Pepi and Laura hire a talented cook and waiter. Their contributions help the bar grow. The cook asks why the owners keep the residual profits if the new workers make much of the growth possible. Laura recognizes her earlier question—now from the employer's side.

**The reversal is not a gotcha:** becoming an owner does not automatically refute criticisms of profit, nor does the voluntary nature of an employment contract settle bargaining power or fairness. Laura may genuinely struggle with the decision and the interests involved.

## Act VI — No es tan sencillo
Return to the three questions:
- **Employment:** initiative, financing, organization, labor, customers, demand and alternative opportunities.
- **Value:** complementary contributions of people, capital, skills and cooperation.
- **Distribution:** property rights, contracts, bargaining, institutions, risk, needs and competing conceptions of justice.

**Provisional closing:**
> «Pepi creó un negocio que no existía. Laura contribuyó a convertirlo en un éxito. Y cuando el negocio creció, otras personas hicieron posibles nuevos resultados.
>
> ¿Quién creó esos puestos de trabajo? ¿Quién generó el valor? ¿Y quién debería quedarse con él?
>
> Son preguntas relacionadas. Pero no son la misma pregunta.
>
> No es tan sencillo».

## Production and editorial notes
- Keep both women recognizable and human, not ideological symbols; respectful but real disagreement should generate tension.
- Avoid treating Pepi's managerial labor as purely passive return to capital.
- Distinguish revenue, costs, compensation for work, cash flow and residual profits.
- Separate the risks of invested capital from risks of lost employment and income.
- Use a clearly labeled fictional economic model; stress-test growth, failure, credit constraints and negotiating power.
- Any factual economic or legal claims must be checked against primary/credible sources before final narration.
- Pair story reveals with evidence and a contradiction review; do not let an elegant story substitute for data.

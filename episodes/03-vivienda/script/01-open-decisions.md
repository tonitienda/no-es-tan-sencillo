# Episode 03 — decisions and open questions

**Last discussion captured:** 2026-11-06.
**Status:** Candidate to release first, ahead of episode 02. [Script v2](04-guion-v2.md) is ready for review, and it isn't ready to record. It runs about 14 minutes at 150 words a minute, not yet read aloud, against the treatment's 10-11; section 7 now answers the four questions and is the longest section.

## Brief from the first discussion
- Topic: housing in Spain. Working folder name `03-vivienda`; episode number still tentative.
- Goal: not to find THE solution. Show that one-sentence answers fall short of a complex problem, without tying the analysis to an ideology.
- Angles requested: population vs new housing; how much can be built (Barcelona, Madrid); empty homes; smaller households; indirect solutions (transport, remote work).

Transport and remote work shrink. **Script v2 cuts them from the narration for time** (see its list of cuts); restore them if the length allows.

## Must verify before recording
5. **Absorption argument: consistent, but not independently confirmed.** The Ministry's method doesn't say how it splits primary and non-primary homes. In 2022-2025 all three official series give about 60% and a gap of 135,000-150,000 homes a year (V24). Over five years the range is about 570,000 to 840,000. The only direct measure found, in the Basque Country, shows absorption in 2019-2021 but not in 2021-2023 (V25).
   - **Decided (2026-11-05):** soften. The closing says the Banco de España estimates this, and calls it a deduction the next census should confirm or refute.
   - **Decided (2026-11-06): don't wait for replies.** The script says only what can be defended without one:
     - "The Banco de España estimates" instead of "this is what happened".
     - "The published method doesn't explain the split; we compared the series" instead of claiming how the Ministry calculates it.
     - The Ministry's non-primary chart is cut, or labelled as an unexplained estimate. No −1.2% or +959,554 on screen.
     - The Basque survey is presented as one region with no published sampling error.
     - Any question to an institution is optional. A reply could improve the script, but nothing waits for one.
6. **Balance of sources on vacancy.** The main "vacancy won't help" sources have industry interests. Vancouver is now the strongest case found for taxing empties, but it is self-reported and also shows the first count was far below estimates (V34). A serious Spanish source is still missing.
7. **Barcelona 9.3% vs 1.22%.** Different years, methods and definitions. Do not imply either is "the real" number until both methods are read. Cut from script v2 for time.
8. **Constitutional Court ruling on Andalusia.** Press says it was struck down on competence grounds, not on the measure itself. Read it.
9. **2026 housing census.** No census after 2021 has published data, and we found no results date. We no longer wait for it; update the video description if it arrives.
10. **How to present the deficit.** The opening uses "750,000", the figure people have heard, and the end explains that it is a subtraction whose size moves with the series. Keep.
11. **Party proposals (V32).** Vox's row now comes from its own 2025 programme, and it corrects our "nothing found" on tourist flats. PSOE's 100% tax is confirmed by PwC, without the parliamentary text. **Checked 2026-11-06:** the PP's site says "construir", so comparing with finished homes is fair, though one report says "promover". The Government's figure is 184,000 at origin, a mix of three sources, known only through press. **Still missing:** the PP's programme PDF, a Government document on the 184,000, Frente Amplio's 2026 proposals and a 2025 EU/non-EU split of foreign purchases. Nothing goes on screen without its original document. Re-check when programmes appear.
12. **Tourist flats (V26-V28).** Barcelona paper read except its tables: the rents are advertised prices up to 2016, and the authors say the effect doesn't explain most of the city's rise. **Checked 2026-11-06:** the Constitutional Court upheld the decree (STC 64/2025), and existing licences can stretch from five to ten years in some cases. Barcelona's plan is an announcement, and the 10,101 is press-only. Look for rent data in Madrid's central districts, where listings fell most, without implying cause.
13. **Rent caps (V29, V36).** The 2023 paper replaces our ~15%: ~10% to ~19% fewer new contracts, depending on the estimator. **A second 2023 paper on the same policy (Jofre-Monseny et al.) finds rents 4-6% lower and no fall in contracts or the rental stock.** Script v2 now gives both and doesn't pick one; we don't yet know why they differ. **Tables read 2026-11-06:** the designs differ (control group, data level, window), and neither paper explains the gap, so the script assigns no cause. Still to do: Fedea's method, and whether seasonal contracts require a deposit, which may explain why portals and deposit data disagree.
14. **Our own scale calculations (V33). Checked 2026-11-06.** The arithmetic holds; the wording did not. "A million barely closes the gap" is true only at about 240,000 households a year, and at about 100,000 a year it would close around 80%. Spain finished more than 250,000 homes every year from 2003 to 2010, so we can't call the target impossible. The treatment now says "at the current pace of households", and that a gap which stays open doesn't prove building fails. Still open: current construction capacity.

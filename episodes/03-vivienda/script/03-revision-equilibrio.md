# Adversarial balance review of script v1

**Date:** 2026-11-06. **Reviewed:** [script v1](02-guion-v1.md), narration and sources. **Applied in:** [script v2](04-guion-v2.md), with its wording tightened for time, so v2 is the reference. The wording below is the original proposal, and the time estimate at the end proved too low.

**Method:** read each section as the hardest honest critic from each side, against [the editorial rules](../../../docs/editorial.md). A finding counts only if the critic could prove it from the script or our own ledger. This is one reviewer's reading, by the same author who drafted the script. It doesn't replace a reader who actually votes differently.

**Verdict:** no invented bias, and the closing passes. But the script doesn't keep its own promise of "the same four questions" for every phrase, and that is the attack most likely to land. The problem runs in both directions, so it isn't a slant towards one party. It is uneven treatment.

## Must fix

### 1. Only one party's promise gets the arithmetic, and we haven't read it
- **Who attacks:** a PP voter, and anyone checking the rules we set ourselves.
- **Evidence:** the PP's million gets the longest test in the video. The ledger marks its text [S], and we don't know if it means started or finished homes (V32). The rule in decision 4 is "attribute each with its document and date". The incumbent's targets get no test: not the 183,000, not the 2% limit or contract extensions in the decrees, not Casa 47. The vacancy and tourist phrases are tested with no party attached, so right-coded ideas get a name and left-coded ones don't.
- **Fix:** adopt one rule: **a party's name goes on a numeric test only when we hold that party's document, and at least one rival's number is tested too.** Until then:

> ¿Cuántas? En esta campaña se ha hablado de un millón en cuatro años. Son 250.000 al año...

  Then, when the PP's text and the origin of the 183,000 are found, name both. V30 already has the sum: 183,000 reaches about 2.4% against about 7%.

### 2. The rent-cap paragraph tilts through a "pero", and drops both sides' best evidence
- **Who attacks:** the left on the first part, the right on the second.
- **Left:** «pero los contratos están en mínimos desde 2010» reads as an effect of the cap. Our own V29 says the fall began in 2021, before the cap of March 2024, that fewer signatures may mean less turnover and not fewer tenants, and that regulated and other municipalities aren't an experiment. The 2020 paper measures new contracts, not the rental stock, and decision 13 says so. The script drops all of it. Rules 4 and 6 apply.
- **Right:** the script omits the strongest evidence for their case, the fall in listings and the shift to seasonal lets (Fedea, idealista, V29). Both have interests, which we can say.
- **Value sentence:** «proteger a quien ya tiene casa frente a quien la busca» is the insider-outsider framing that critics of caps prefer. A supporter would frame it as tenant stability against landlord income.
- **Names:** we hold Vox's text on repealing interventionist measures [P] and the decrees [P], yet no name appears. The table's fallback is "cite only Vox", which would itself be uneven. Name both or neither.
- **Fix:**

> Con el tope actual, los municipios regulados suben un 0,7% frente al 13,8% del resto, aunque no son municipios comparables. Los contratos firmados están en mínimos desde 2010, con una caída que empezó antes del tope, y menos firmas también puede ser menos rotación. Los anuncios caen más, y crece el alquiler de temporada, según datos del propio sector. Nadie ha publicado aún una evaluación causal de este tope.

> Cuánto cuesta es un dato. Cómo repartir ese coste entre quien ya vive de alquiler, quien lo busca y quien es propietario es un valor.

### 3. The scale test changes between phrases
- **Who attacks:** anyone who favours limiting tourist flats or using empty homes.
- **Evidence:** a million homes is measured **net** of new households: 40,000 over four years at 240,000 a year. Tourist flats are measured **gross**: 341,001, or 45% of the deficit. On the script's own net basis, converting every tourist flat yields about 8.5 times the margin of the million. The script then calls the tourist figure «poco» and never tests the 400,000 empty homes in large cities, which would be 53% of the deficit before caveats.
- **«Poco» appears twice** as a verdict, in Madrid and nationally. Nearly half the deficit isn't self-evidently little, and no other phrase gets that word.
- **Fix:** put every option on one basis. Say what each is worth against the 750,000, then say which are one-off and which recur:

> Aunque todas pasaran a vivienda habitual, serían cerca del 45% del déficit, y solo una vez.

> Las dos cosas son ciertas. Para la cuenta nacional, una sola vez y como máximo. Para un barrio concreto, quizá lo que más importa.

  In Madrid, drop «Poco.» and let the nine months stand. Add to vacías: «Si todas sirvieran, serían algo más de la mitad del déficit. Pero el dato no separa un piso cerrado de una ruina.» Consider making the closing table show this column.

### 4. The vacancy section is the weakest test, and looks it
- **Who attacks:** the left.
- **Evidence:** it is the shortest main section (176 words against 301 and 378), answers one question of four, and leaves out the Spanish measures the treatment included: the Catalan tax, the Andalusian expropriation and the IBI surcharge. Its "pro" case is a foreign example closing on «pero». The first count finding a tenth of the estimate speaks to the estimate, not to whether the tax works. Construction gets a sentence protecting it from exactly that kind of inference.
- **Fix:** add the matching sentence, and add «¿para quién?» and «¿para cuándo?»:

> Que el primer recuento fallara dice que la estimación era mala, no que el impuesto no funcione.

  Use the IBI surcharge once we have read that part of the RDL 29. Andalusia stays out until the ruling is read.

### 5. Public housing gets no precedent, though construction does
- **Who attacks:** the left, and the right gets something too.
- **Evidence:** construction hears «¿Es imposible? No», with 2003-2010. The treatment's «acierta en a quién llega» was lost, and public housing ends on sceptical questions. The Banco de España reports over 1.3 million protected homes built with public funds in 1991-2015, mostly sold below market, on time-limited protection (Informe Anual 2025, PDF p. 177). That answers both sides: it has been done at scale, and most of it left the public stock.
- **Fix:**

> ¿Es imposible? España construyó más de 1,3 millones de viviendas protegidas entre 1991 y 2015, según el Banco de España. Pero la mayoría se vendieron, y la protección tenía fecha de caducidad. Por eso hoy el parque es tan pequeño. Hacerlas no basta: importa qué pasa después, con qué dinero y en qué suelo.

  Add it to the ledger under V7 before use.

### 6. We disclose some interests and not the main witness's position
- **Who attacks:** the left, and any careful reader.
- **Evidence:** the Banco de España is named seven times and acts as referee. Its report says policy should prioritise more new homes (PDF p. 17). That's a legitimate position, but a stated one. We disclose the developers' chair, the employers' group and the city council. We don't disclose that the 2020 rent paper had Generalitat support (V29). And «distintos intereses» in Barcelona implies the INE census has an interest.
- **Fix:**

> Distintos años y distintos métodos, y una de las dos llega citada por quien tiene interés en el asunto.

  In section 8 or section 4: «El Banco de España, que recomienda priorizar la vivienda nueva, estima...». Add the Generalitat's support to the description, or say it aloud if the rent section keeps its time.

### 7. Vox appears only through nationality
- **Who attacks:** a Vox voter, and a fair-minded opponent.
- **Evidence:** both Vox mentions concern foreigners or Spanish families. Its programme also proposes more land, height and density and lower taxes on building (V32, read at source), and that's left out of the one section where it applies. «Familias españolas» also carries no quotation marks while «inundar» does, so it reads as our gloss. Meanwhile Sumar is cited with a 2023 figure and Podemos and ERC not at all.
- **Fix:** in «¿Dónde?»:

> Vox propone, en su programa de 2025, más suelo y más altura. Sumar y otros partidos, rehabilitar. Es justo aquí donde importa cuánto suelo hay y quién decide cómo se usa.

  Check Sumar's line against a text first. In public housing, quote the phrase whole: «Vox propone "inundar España de bloques de vivienda protegida para las familias españolas"». That's its wording, p. 44-46 of the programme. Seek one left-side test beyond Sumar, such as Podemos on expropriating funds, once checked at source.

### 8. The topics each side will say we dodged
- **Who attacks:** both, and this is the most likely comment under the video.
- **Evidence:** in the narration, «salario», «ingresos» and «grandes tenedores» appear zero times. Neither do squatting, unpaid rent, licence delays or construction taxes. Prices appear once. «¿Cuántas?» as the first question favours measures that produce homes; rent caps and taxes can't score on it. The left will say the crisis is prices against wages and who owns the stock. The right will say it is legal insecurity, red tape and tax.
- **We have no verified data on these,** and the deadline for verification is 11 November.
- **Fix, choose one:**
  - **A, recommended:** one research pass for two figures, one per side: price or rent against income, and licence delays or eviction times. One sentence each.
  - **B:** state the limit on screen and in narration:

> No hemos hablado de salarios, de quién es dueño de las viviendas, de impagos ni de licencias. Importan, y merecen su propio vídeo. Aquí solo probamos las frases de la campaña.

### 9. The decree variants carry different tones
- **Evidence:** variant A says «el decreto en vigor», with no author. Variant B says «el Gobierno lo intentó», which reads as a failure. If it falls, the critic also asks who voted how.
- **Fix:** use one sentence and fill in the outcome, with who voted how if the record is clear:

> Y prohibir no es lo mismo que encarecer. El Gobierno aprobó en octubre un decreto que sube el IVA al 10% y permite recargos del IBI en zonas tensionadas. La Diputación Permanente [lo convalidó / no lo convalidó].

## Lower priority
- **Opening phrases are ours.** They carry no attribution, and «evidente» and «han echado» are sharp. Rule 2 says no caricature. Label them on screen as «frases resumidas por nosotros», or find a real example per phrase from more than one side.
- **PSOE's tax:** experts' doubts about confiscation and capital freedom (V32) are left out. So is any test of Vox's measure, because its text gives no detail. Add a word on the legal doubt, attributed.
- **«Quien cita solo una de estas cifras no miente»** is the only use of that word, and only rent gets it. It works there. Keep it, knowing the script otherwise avoids the frame.
- **Transport and remote work:** the treatment's point that a better train partly raises prices at that stop was lost. Cut or restore it, as it's first on the cut list anyway.

## What passed
- **Closing:** «sea quien sea» and the list of value questions work for every voter. «Cuánto limitar la propiedad» sits fairly next to «a quién proteger».
- **No voting advice:** the opening's disclaimer holds up through the script.
- **PSOE and Vox on foreign buyers:** «no son la misma medida» prevents false equivalence.
- **Employers and city council:** «ninguna de las dos cifras es independiente» treats both alike.
- **Section 8:** it gives the vacancy side a real point and names Arellano and Roca against the deficit, so the video doesn't end on the supply side.
- **Section 3:** no party angle found.

## Time cost
The fixes add about 130 to 170 words, or about a minute, on a script at about 11.2 minutes. Cutting transport and remote work, and tightening section 3, returns about a third of that. Over 12 minutes, cut within sections evenly. The treatment's rule stands: never cut one phrase and keep another.

## What this review can't settle
- Items 1 and 7 depend on documents we don't hold: the PP's text and 2026 programmes.
- Fixes 4, 5 and 7 need the checks they name before recording.
- Wording tilt is partly a matter of ear. **Before locking, ask two or three readers who vote differently to flag the sentence that annoyed them most.**
